Calculations

Normal range

The normal range is 1991–2020, the current WMO standard reference period. For daily variables, the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles use every day within ±7 days of the calendar date across the 30 years. For rainfall since sowing, each 1991–2020 season is accumulated from its own sowing date, and percentiles are taken across seasons for each day after sowing.

Crop season and stages

Sowing and maturity dates come from GGCMI for the 0.5° cell. The four FAO-56 stages (initial, development, mid-season, late season) take their proportions from one FAO-56 Table 11 row per crop, scaled to the local season length. The row used is shown on the page and listed in public/data/crop_params.json.

Crop water need

Crop evapotranspiration ETc = Kc × ET₀ (FAO-56 single crop coefficient method). Kc is constant in the initial stage, rises linearly to Kcmid over development, stays at Kcmid through mid-season, and changes linearly to Kcend over the late season. Where FAO-56 gives a range, the midpoint is used. ET₀ is the FAO-56 Penman–Monteith reference evapotranspiration computed by Open-Meteo from ERA5. Effective rainfall uses the USDA Soil Conservation Service formula as implemented in FAO CROPWAT: Peff = P(125 − 0.2P)/125 for P ≤ 250 mm per month, otherwise 125 + 0.1P, applied per calendar month and pro-rated for partial months. The shortfall is the sum over months of max(0, ETc − Peff). Rapeseed and rye have no FAO-56 stage row, so no water need is computed for them.

Heat thresholds

Maize 29 °C, soybean 30 °C and cotton 32 °C (Schlenker & Roberts 2009, PNAS 106:15594); rice 35 °C at flowering (Yoshida 1981); wheat 34 °C (Lobell et al. 2012, Nature Climate Change 2:186). The page counts days with daily maximum above the threshold. This is a simpler measure than the hourly exposure used by Schlenker & Roberts. Other crops have no threshold shown.

ENSO episodes and local composites

An El Niño (La Niña) episode needs at least five consecutive overlapping 3-month seasons at or above +0.5 °C (at or below −0.5 °C), following NOAA CPC. A crop season counts as El Niño or La Niña when more than half its days fall in an episode month, otherwise as neutral. Season rainfall is expressed as a percentage departure from the mean of the 1991–2020 seasons. Season temperature is the mean daily maximum, shown as a departure from a linear trend fitted over 1950–present, so warming is not mistaken for an ENSO effect. The difference between El Niño and neutral seasons is tested with a two-sided rank-based permutation test (Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney statistic, 10,000 permutations, fixed seed), so a single extreme season cannot dominate the result. The page only says "El Niño seasons here were drier/wetter" when p < 0.05. Where there is no crop calendar, the six months starting in the selected month are used instead.

Guidance

Guidance is rule-based. Each item appears only when its data trigger is met. The page shows the trigger and the source:

  • rain since sowing below the 20th percentile with a shortfall above 25 mm (FAO-33);
  • root-zone soil moisture below the 10th percentile for the time of year;
  • ensemble-median Tmax above the crop threshold during mid-season in the next 15 days;
  • ensemble-median rain of 75 mm or more over three days;
  • SEAS5 ensemble-mean rainfall at least 20% below the model normal in a month overlapping mid-season (during the season) or the coming season (before sowing);
  • an El Niño run in progress: a pointer to the local El Niño history, and once it is loaded, a note if past El Niño seasons were drier (significant, or at least 60% of them below normal with the caveat stated).

No guidance is given for past dates.

Limitations

  • ERA5 is a ~31 km model reanalysis. Rainfall is least reliable in steep terrain and convective climates, and it cannot represent a single field.
  • Harvested area counts multiple cropping. Crop maps are circa 2020 and calendars are static, so crop mixes and sowing dates in past decades may have been different.
  • FAO-56 ETc describes a healthy, well-watered crop. Rice paddy water use (land preparation, percolation, standing water) is higher than ETc.
  • Seasonal forecasts are not bias-corrected and have limited skill at one location.
  • ENSO composites describe past seasons. The number of El Niño seasons since 1950 is small, and individual events differ.
  • Open-Meteo's free tier is for non-commercial use and is rate-limited: 600 weighted calls per minute, 5,000 per hour and 10,000 per day, counted per visitor IP. To stay within it, the page downloads only the 1991–2020 date windows a view needs (each crop season, and the weeks around the selected date) rather than the full record, paces its own requests, and caches everything it has downloaded in the browser.
